# Municipal Structures Act 117 of 1998, Schedule 1 Items 12-16: exact wording (for the 4 Nov 2026 elections)

Method note: text below was extracted by me from primary PDFs (pdftotext) and quoted verbatim. Where a sentence is my own it is marked as inference. The words "remainder" does not appear in the Act: the Act says "surplus".

Source reliability grading used here:
- A = Act/Bill as published (Government Gazette PDF or Parliament/PMG copy of it).
- B = LawLibrary/AfricanLII reproduction of the Gazette text with amendments applied (not an official publication, but states it reproduces the Gazette text).
- No current (post-2021) consolidated text of Items 12-16 could be retrieved (see Gaps). Item 13 below is from a B-grade 2009 consolidation, corroborated as unchanged by A-grade amendment history.

## (a) Verbatim text of Item 13, incl. the equal-surplus (tie) rule

### Takeaway
Item 13(2)(b) exists and reads as the secondary source reported: if the surplus for two or more parties is equal, the seat goes to the party that obtained the highest number of valid votes. Item 13 contains no lot, no Commission discretion, and no rule for equal votes.

### Cited Findings
- Source of text: LawLibrary/AfricanLII PDF, "Local Government: Municipal Structures Act, 1998 (Act 117 of 1998), Legislation as at 17 April 2009" (this is the version that has Items 12-16 in their post-2002/2008 form; it predates Act 3 of 2021) — [lawlib PDF](https://media.lawlibrary.org.za/media/legislation/299115/source_file/df3b46e236e79c40/1998-117.pdf). Note the PDF itself says "This is not the latest available version of this Act."
- Item 13 verbatim (Schedule 1, Part 1, "Allocating seats"):

  > 13. Allocating seats
  > (1) (a) The total number of valid votes cast for each party on the party vote and for the ward candidates representing the party must be divided by the quota of votes for a seat. The result is the total number of seats to which each party is entitled before any adjustment in terms of subitem (3).
  > (b) If a ward candidate representing a party is elected unopposed, a vote cast by a voter registered in that ward for the party of which that candidate is a representative must for the purpose of paragraph (a) be counted as two votes.
  > (2) (a) If the calculation in subitem (1) yields a surplus not absorbed by the seats awarded to a party, that surplus must compete with similar surpluses accruing to any other party or parties, and any undistributed seat or seats must be awarded to the party or parties concerned in sequence of the highest surplus.
  > (b) If the surplus for two or more parties is equal the seat must be awarded to the party that obtained the highest number of valid votes.
  > (3) (a) In an election for a metro council or for a local council that has wards, the Electoral Commission must deduct from the total number of seats to which each party is entitled in terms of subitem (1) and (2), the number of ward candidates representing that party who were declared elected.
  > (b) The result is the number of seats which the party is entitled to fill from its list of party candidates.
  > (4) If no party is awarded a seat in terms of subitem (1), the votes for each party, read with subitem (1)(b), must be treated in accordance with subitem (2) as if they are surpluses.
  > (5) The Electoral Commission must determine which party candidates are elected by selecting from the party's list, in accordance with the party's order of preference on the list, the number of candidates that is equal to the number of seats to which the party is entitled, beginning with the first candidate on the list and ending with the lowest ranking candidate.

  (Source annotation: "[item 13 amended by section 23 of Act 51 of 2002]".) Same PDF as above. (The PDF text has "fora local council"; I have rendered it "for a local council", a spacing artefact of the extraction.)
- Independent A-grade corroboration that Item 13(2) has exactly paragraphs (a) and (b) as at January 2025: the Municipal Structures Second Amendment Bill B9-2025 proposes "the addition of the following paragraph in item 13(2): '(c) Only a party who has already obtained at least one seat as per subitem (1)(a) may compete with similar surpluses as per paragraphs (a) and (b).'" — [B9-2025 (PMG)](https://static.pmg.org.za/B9-2025-GJ.pdf). Its heading lists the full amendment history of Schedule 1 as: Act 27 of 2000 s93, Act 20 of 2002 s8, Act 51 of 2002 ss22-26, Act 2 of 2003 s9, Act 55 of 2008 s16 and Act 3 of 2021 s33.
- Post-2021 parallel wording for the same rule (new Item 16(4)(b)-(c), inserted by Act 3 of 2021 s33(a), verbatim): "(b) If the calculation in paragraph (a) yields a surplus not absorbed by the seats awarded to a party, that surplus must compete with similar surpluses accruing to any other party or parties and any undistributed seat or seats must be awarded to the party or parties concerned in sequence of the highest surplus. (c) If the surplus for two or more parties is equal, the seat must be awarded to the party that received the highest number of valid votes." — [Act 3 of 2021 (PMG PDF)](https://static.pmg.org.za/Act_3_of_2021_Municipal_Structures.pdf) (also [Parliament copy](https://www.parliament.gov.za/storage/app/media/Acts/2021/Act_3_of_2021_Municipal_Structures_Amendment_Act.pdf)).
- Secondary source (not relied on): web summary of lawlibrary text describing the same tie-break — [IEC/LawLibrary search result](https://lawlibrary.org.za/akn/za/act/1998/117/eng@2022-11-01); I could not open that page (Cloudflare 403).

### Inferences
- The exact wording of 13(2)(b) differs slightly from the reported paraphrase: "obtained the highest number of valid votes" (13(2)(b)) vs "received" (16(4)(c)). Tie-break is on the party's valid votes, not on the surplus.
- It is unspecified in 13(2)(b) whether "valid votes" means party-vote plus ward-candidate votes (the 13(1)(a) total) or party vote only. The text says only "highest number of valid votes". Treat the 13(1)(a) basis as an interpretation, not a quotation. Needs attorney/IEC confirmation.

## (b) Ties: votes, lot, Commission, or silent?

### Takeaway
Equal surpluses are broken by votes: the party with the highest number of valid votes gets the seat (Item 13(2)(b)). The Act text I read says nothing about lot, nothing about Commission discretion, and nothing about what happens if the vote totals are also equal.

### Cited Findings
- Tie-break is by valid votes — Item 13(2)(b) quoted above, [lawlib PDF](https://media.lawlibrary.org.za/media/legislation/299115/source_file/df3b46e236e79c40/1998-117.pdf).
- Search of the 2009 consolidated text and of Act 3 of 2021 (English text) for "lot" / drawing of lots in Schedule 1 produced no tie mechanism in Items 12-16 other than the 13(2)(b) and 16(4)(c) rules — [Act 3 of 2021](https://static.pmg.org.za/Act_3_of_2021_Municipal_Structures.pdf). (I searched for "equal", "surplus", "highest"; I did not read every line of the 2009 Act outside Schedule 1. Other Schedule 1 items, e.g. ward-election ties, are outside this scope.)

### Inferences
- Because the Act is silent on a second-level tie (equal surplus AND equal votes), the Commission's general powers would apply, but I found no text saying so. Do not code a lot-draw or a Commission rule as statute.

### Gaps
- No text found for equal surplus and equal votes. Not verified whether the IEC has a published procedure; the IEC seat-calculation deck I found ([IEC PDF](https://www.elections.org.za/content/Documents/Voter-education/Continuous-Voter-Education/How-Council-seats-are-calculated/)) did not address ties in what I extracted.

## (c) Verbatim text of Item 16 (excessive seats) and Items 12, 14, 15

### Takeaway
Item 16(1) says a party whose ward seats are equal to or greater than its Item 13 entitlement "must not be allocated any seats from its list of party candidates"; 16(2) leaves ward seats untouched. Act 3 of 2021 (in force 1 November 2021) added 16(3)-(9): a recalculation of the quota excluding that party.

### Cited Findings
- Item 16 as at 2009 (verbatim), [lawlib PDF](https://media.lawlibrary.org.za/media/legislation/299115/source_file/df3b46e236e79c40/1998-117.pdf):

  > 16. Excessive seats
  > (1) If, through the election of ward candidates, a party listed on the part of the ballot paper for parties has obtained a number of seats that is equal to, or greater than the total number of seats in the council to which it is entitled under item 13, that party must not be allocated any seats from its list of party candidates.
  > (2) The seats of ward candidates are not affected.
- Items 16(3)-(9) added by Act 3 of 2021 s33(a) (verbatim, English text; formula is a typeset image in the PDF text extraction, shown as extracted: "A–B +1 over C – (D + E)"), [Act 3 of 2021](https://static.pmg.org.za/Act_3_of_2021_Municipal_Structures.pdf):

  > (3) A new quota of votes for a seat must be determined in accordance with the following formula (fractions to be disregarded): [formula: (A − B) / (C − (D + E)), then "+1" — see Gap] Where— A represents the total number of valid votes cast for all parties, consisting of those cast on the party vote and those cast for ward candidates representing parties; B represents the total number of votes cast for the party with excessive seats, both on the party vote and for ward candidates representing parties; C represents the number of seats in the council; D represents the number of seats awarded to the party with excessive seats; and E represents the number of independent ward councillors elected.
  > (4) (a) The total number of valid votes cast for each party, both on the party vote and for ward candidates representing the party, excluding the party that has excessive number of seats, must be divided by the quota of votes for a seat. The result is the total number of seats to which each party is entitled. (b) [surplus competition, quoted in (a) above] (c) If the surplus for two or more parties is equal, the seat must be awarded to the party that received the highest number of valid votes.
  > (5) If a ward candidate representing a party is elected unopposed, a vote cast by a voter registered in that ward for the party of which that candidate is a representative must, for the purpose of factors A and B and subitem (4), be counted as two votes.
  > (6) In an election for a council that has wards, the Electoral Commission must deduct from the total number of seats to which each party is entitled in terms of subitem (4), the number of ward candidates representing the party who were declared elected.
  > (7) If no party is awarded a seat in terms of subitem (4)(a), the votes for each party must be treated in accordance with subitem (4)(b) as if they are surpluses.
  > (8) The Electoral Commission must determine in the manner provided in item 13(5), which party candidates are elected.
  > (9) If a party is entitled to an additional number of seats in terms of subitem (4) and its list of candidates does not contain a sufficient number of candidates after having applied item 17(1), the party concerned forfeits, subject to subitem (1), the unfilled seats and the process provided in this item must be repeated until all seats have been filled or until all listed candidates have been allocated to a vacant seat.
- Item 12 (Quota) as at 2009 (verbatim, formula image not captured in text): "(1) The quota of votes for a seat in the metro or local council, must be determined in accordance with the following formula (fractions to be disregarded): [formula] Where— A represents the total number of valid votes cast for all parties, consisting of those cast on the party vote and those cast for ward candidates representing parties; B represents, the number of seats in the metro or local council; and C represents the number of independent ward councillors elected in the election. (2) If a ward candidate representing a party is elected unopposed, a vote cast by a voter registered in that ward for the party of which that candidate is a representative must for the purpose of factor A be counted as two votes." — [lawlib PDF](https://media.lawlibrary.org.za/media/legislation/299115/source_file/df3b46e236e79c40/1998-117.pdf)
- Item 14 (Uncontested elections) as at 2009: "(1) If only one party submitted a list, an election according to proportional representation must not be held for the metro or local council. (2) The number of seats to which the party is entitled is the total number of seats on the council to be filled by proportional representation. (3) The chief electoral officer must determine which party candidates are elected by selecting from the party's list, according to the party's order of preference on the list, the number of candidates that is equal to the number of seats to which the party is entitled." — same source.
- Item 15 (Procedure if no party applications) as at 2009: (1) by-election within 90 days of nomination day, MEC determines date after consulting the Commission; (2) if no party applies or every application is rejected: municipal manager notifies chief electoral officer within 24 hours, chief electoral officer within seven days determines date for further election within 90 days, vacancies remain unfilled until filled by election; (3) "Section 25 applies to a by-election in terms of this item, to the extent that that section can be applied." — same source. (Paraphrase of sub-items (1)-(2); the source has verbatim wording and annotations I did not reproduce in full.)

### Inferences
- A party at or over its entitlement gets no list seats: 16(1) says so in terms ("equal to, or greater than"). Its ward seats stay (16(2)). Under 16(3)-(9) the remaining list seats are then re-allocated among the other parties with a new quota; the excessive party is excluded from the 16(4)(a) division.
- Item 12 in the text I read has only factors A, B, C, giving (A/(B−C))+1; it has no factor D. The build discipline note states Item 12 as `Q = (A/(B−C−D))+1`. I did not find a factor D in Item 12 in any version I read, and Act 3 of 2021 does not amend Item 12 (see below). This needs a check against the formula image in the Gazette before the calculator relies on it. The "+1" in the formula is in the formula image; I did not read the image in the 2009 PDF, so even the 2009 formula is not verified by me.

### Gaps
- The Item 12 and Item 16(3) formulas are typeset images/graphics; the text extraction does not give an authoritative rendering. Verify visually against the Gazette PDF.

## Amendments to Schedule 1 Items 12-16, and effect date

### Takeaway
Only one Act changes these items since 2009: Local Government: Municipal Structures Amendment Act 3 of 2021, s33(a), which adds sub-items 16(3)-(9) and does not touch Items 12-15. It came into operation on 1 November 2021. No later amendment found; two pending bills do not alter Items 12-16.

### Cited Findings
- Act 3 of 2021 s33 amends Schedule 1 only by: (a) adding 16(3)-(9); (b) substituting 17(1); (c) inserting 17A; (d)-(e) item 18(1)(b) and new (c); (f) item 20(1). No change to Items 12, 13, 14 or 15 — [Act 3 of 2021](https://static.pmg.org.za/Act_3_of_2021_Municipal_Structures.pdf). (Section 33 also amends Schedule 1 only; its Schedule 2 changes, s34, concern district councils.)
- Commencement: section 39 "comes into operation on a date to be determined by the President by proclamation" (same PDF). Proclamation: 1 November 2021, Gazette 45305 of 11 October 2021 — [gov.za](https://www.gov.za/documents/local-government-municipal-structures-amendment-act-3-2021-1-jun-2021-0000). Corruption Watch headline "Municipal Structures Amendment Act to commence on 1 November" and a search summary say section 30 was excluded from that commencement — [Corruption Watch](https://www.corruptionwatch.org.za/municipal-structures-amendment-act-to-commence/) (page timed out for me; I relied on the search-result summary for the section 30 exception). Section 30 concerns the code of conduct for traditional leaders, not Schedule 1.

Status: PMG listing via search result, not independently confirmed.

## (d) Any Item dealing with a tie that cannot be broken by votes (equal votes)?

### Takeaway
None found. Neither Item 13(2)(b) nor Item 16(4)(c) provides a fallback if the parties' valid votes are also equal.

### Cited Findings
- 13(2)(b) and 16(4)(c) both stop at "the highest number of valid votes" — see quotes above.
- Items 14 and 15 deal with uncontested elections and no-list situations, not ties.

### Inferences
- A calculator should treat a full tie (equal surplus and equal valid votes) as a case the Act does not resolve: flag it, do not invent a lot-draw.

### Gaps
- Whether a regulation, IEC directive or Commission procedure fills this is not established; not searched beyond the IEC explainer deck.
