# Tied remainders in SA council seat allocation: IEC practice, courts, documented instances

Scope: South Africa, local government elections 2000-2026. Primary vs secondary sources are labelled. Searches were limited (about 12 tool calls); several pages could not be fetched (noted under Gaps).

## 1. Has the IEC published a document stating the tie-break rule for equal remainders?

### Takeaway
No IEC document found that states a tie-break for equal remainders. The IEC's 2021 Electoral System deck says only "in order of highest remainder" and cites Schedule 1. The only text on an equal-surplus tie in Item 13 is statutory (quoted by the Constitutional Court): equal surpluses go to the party with the highest number of valid votes, not to lot.

### Cited Findings
- IEC 2021 LGE "Electoral System" deck (PDF on elections.org.za), slide "Seat Calculation - Metro and local councils", Step 2: "Divide the valid votes for each party (ward and PR) by the quota to get the initial allocation. Any remaining seats are allocated in order of the highest remainder to get the total number of seats for each party." The slide says "Schedule 1 of Municipal Structures Act, 117 of 1998 describes the seat allocation process." Same wording for district councils (40% and 60%): "Any remaining seats are awarded in the order of highest remainder." A keyword search of the whole deck text for "tie", "equal", "lot" found nothing relevant to remainders. — [IEC, How Council seats are calculated (2021 LGE Electoral System)](https://www.elections.org.za/content/Documents/Voter-education/Continuous-Voter-Education/How-Council-seats-are-calculated/)
- The statutory tie rule appears in a Constitutional Court footnote quoting Item 13(2) of Schedule 1: "(a) If the calculation in sub-item (1) yields a surplus not absorbed by the seats awarded to a party, that surplus must compete with similar surpluses accruing to any other party or parties, and any undistributed seat or seats must be awarded to the party or parties concerned in sequence of the highest surplus. (b) If the surplus for two or more parties is equal the seat must be awarded to the party that obtained the highest number of valid votes." — [SADA v Minister of CoGTA [2025] ZACC 26, fn 6](https://collections.concourt.org.za/bitstreams/e2ecb4ca-73ae-43a7-87d6-6fc6f7e44caf/download) (primary; the judgment text was extracted from the PDF locally)
- The same footnote quotes Item 16(4)(b) (the district/other-seat equivalent) with only the highest-surplus sentence, no tie sentence in the quoted text. — same source
- IEC "Seat Calculation Detail" per-municipality reports exist on the results site (for example the Cape Town and EC131 PDFs); search snippets describe the same quota/highest-remainder method. I did not open them. — [example: CPT Seat Calculation Detail](https://www.elections.org.za/content/LGEPublicReports/402/Seat%20Calculation%20Detail/WP/CPT.pdf)

### Inferences
- The IEC's published explainer is a simplification that omits the Item 13(2)(b) tie-break. This is an inference from the deck text lacking any tie wording, not a statement by the IEC.
- Item 13(2)(b) as quoted by the Court (votes, not lot) differs from the lot rule in s 43(2)(c) and Item 8(2). Whether Item 13(2)(b) is the current enacted text should be checked against the consolidated Act, because the Court only quoted it.

### Gaps
- No regulation, directive, party liaison committee paper or results-system specification stating a remainder tie-break was found or opened.
- Consolidated Act text on gov.za/SAFLII could not be fetched (503), so Item 13(2)(b) is confirmed only via the Court's quotation.
- Seat Calculation Detail PDFs, the 2024 NPE seat calculation page and the IEC FAQ were not opened.

## 2. Court decisions or comments on a tie on remainders or on Item 13

### Takeaway
One directly relevant decision: Socialist Agenda of Dispossessed Africans v Minister of Cooperative Governance and Traditional Affairs [2025] ZACC 26 (CCT 279/23, 20 November 2025, Majiedt J, unanimous). It concerns lot for equal surpluses on the executive committee (s 43(2)(c)), not council seats, but it quotes Item 13(2)(b) and treats the votes-based tie-break for council seats as deliberate. No court decision on an actual tie in a Schedule 1 remainder was found.

### Cited Findings
- Facts: Fetakgomo Tubatse (Limpopo), 2021 LGE; 77 seats; DA and SADA each won 2 seats, so surpluses were equal for the 10-member executive committee; the last seat was decided by lot, won by the DA, although SADA had more votes (3 667 v 3 321). — [ZACC 26 paras 4-8](https://collections.concourt.org.za/bitstreams/e2ecb4ca-73ae-43a7-87d6-6fc6f7e44caf/download)
- Holding: the High Court's declaration that s 43(2)(c) is inconsistent with ss 19(3)(a) and 160(8) of the Constitution was not confirmed; lot is "a robust and seemingly peculiar mechanism chosen by Parliament" but does not violate the Constitution (para 45; order not confirmed, paras 44-50). — same
- On method: "The Act does not specify the exact procedure for casting lots. Similar processes typically involve random draws conducted openly and transparently, e.g. physical lot-drawing, such as the drawing of numbers or names from a container." (para 25) — same
- The Court contrasted elective bodies: "the Legislature clearly contemplated the possibility of breaking deadlocks by having regard to votes cast and that this was the method chosen for elective (legislative) bodies", citing "Schedule 1 items 13(2), 16(4) and 17(5) and Schedule 2 items 7(2), 10(5) and 20(2)" (para 46 and fn 33). — same
- The Court lists where the Act uses lot: Item 8(2) of Schedule 1 (equal votes between ward candidates: "determined by lot") and Schedule 3 (office-bearer elections). — para 40, same
- The High Court (Gauteng, Pretoria, Case 4646/2022, 10 October 2023, unreported) had held that where "all variables are not equal there is, strictly speaking, no tie" and lot is only fair when votes are exactly equal. — described at paras 10-11, 47-48, same
- Electoral Court 2004 judgment on national highest-remainder allocation (Parliament, not municipal), noted by search snippet only: [SAFLII ZAEC 2004/2](https://www.saflii.org/za/cases/ZAEC/2004/2.pdf) (could not be fetched, 403).
- Academic Congress Union v IEC, Case 006/22EC, [2022] ZAEC 10, 6 July 2022: seat count discrepancy in a KZN municipality (26 v 25 seats). Snippet only; PDF text not readable; no tie mentioned in what I could see. — [SCA site copy](https://www.supremecourtofappeal.org.za/index.php/component/jdownloads/send/47-judgments-2022/3877-academic-congress-union-v-independent-electoral-commission-case-no-006-22ec-2022-zaec-10-06-july-2022?Itemid=0)
- Electoral Court on Schedule 2 Item 20 (district council seats, uMhlathuze), snippet only, no tie holding seen: [Dullah Omar Institute bulletin](https://dullahomarinstitute.org.za/multilevel-govt/local-government-bulletin/archives/volume-18-issue-4-november-december-2023/court-rules-on-effect-of-walk-out-on-district-elections) and [King Cetshwayo ConCourt news](https://m.motoring.co.za/news/politics/anc-set-to-get-three-more-seats-at-king-cetshwayo-district-municipality-after-concourt-ruling-cb6bee87-9e99-472b-b111-55c720efa93c) (secondary).

### Inferences
- The ConCourt's paraphrase supports reading Item 13(2)(b) as a votes-based, not lot-based, tie-break, but the point was obiter; the court did not decide a remainder tie.

### Gaps
- The Electoral Court 2004 and 2022 judgments were not read. No SAFLII search of "equal remainder" or Item 13 across Electoral Court judgments was completed.

## 3. Documented real instances of tied remainders in a municipal election

### Takeaway
None found. The documented ties are other kinds: ward ties (resolved by lot, Item 8(2)), a mayoral tie, and an executive-committee surplus tie.

### Cited Findings
- Executive committee surplus tie (not council seats), Fetakgomo Tubatse, 23 Nov 2021: resolved by lot by the council, DA won. — [ZACC 26 para 8](https://collections.concourt.org.za/bitstreams/e2ecb4ca-73ae-43a7-87d6-6fc6f7e44caf/download) (primary)
- Ward tie, Mutale (Limpopo), 2011: independent candidate and ANC candidate each 823 votes; after recount the IEC resorted to a coin toss; the provincial electoral officer said the Act required lots; the independent refused to take part. — [Business Report / The Star](https://busrep.co.za/the-star/soweto/ward-candidate-loses-by-flip-of-a-coin-1073262) (secondary; page itself failed to load, relied on search summary)
- Ward tie, Ditsobotla (Lichtenburg), 2006, decided by coin toss "as legally prescribed" — search summary only; URL not identified (secondary, unverified).
- Mayoral tie, Modimolle-Mookgophong, Dec 2021: decided by coin toss under Schedule 3. — [Pretoria News](https://pretorianews.co.za/news/2021-12-06-da-takes-mayoral-position-in-modimolle-mookgophong-through-coin-toss/) (secondary)

### Inferences
- Absence of reports is not evidence a remainder tie never happened; ties on exact remainders are rare and a votes-based tie-break would not be newsworthy.

### Gaps
- No search of IEC objection/recount records or Electoral Court roll for remainder ties was possible within the budget.

## 4. Does the Commission's results system or public explainers say who decides a tie (Commission, returning officer, lot)?

### Takeaway
Nothing found. IEC explainers and the seat-calculation reports reviewed say "highest remainder" with no tie language. Only in ward ties does a source (secondary) say the Commission conducted the lot.

### Cited Findings
- IEC deck: see section 1; no mention of ties, lot or who decides. — [IEC deck](https://www.elections.org.za/content/Documents/Voter-education/Continuous-Voter-Education/How-Council-seats-are-calculated/)
- Mutale 2011 ward tie: the IEC itself carried out the coin toss (secondary). — [Business Report](https://busrep.co.za/the-star/soweto/ward-candidate-loses-by-flip-of-a-coin-1073262)
- The ConCourt notes the Act sets no lot procedure (para 25). — [ZACC 26](https://collections.concourt.org.za/bitstreams/e2ecb4ca-73ae-43a7-87d6-6fc6f7e44caf/download)

### Inferences
- None beyond the gaps.

### Gaps
- IEC results-system specifications, Results Operation Centre manuals, party liaison committee minutes and electoral regulations were not located. The IEC FAQ and 2024 seat-calculation pages were not opened.
