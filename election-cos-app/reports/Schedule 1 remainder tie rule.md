# Equal surpluses go to votes, and the Act stops there

For the 4 November 2026 elections, Schedule 1 of the Municipal Structures Act 117 of 1998 settles a tie between equal surpluses by votes: under Item 13(2)(b), "the seat must be awarded to the party that obtained the highest number of valid votes". It gives no lot, no Commission discretion and no rule for the case where surplus and votes are both equal, so that last case is unresolved in the statute. The Item 12 quota is A / (B − C) + 1 with fractions disregarded, where A is total valid votes (party vote plus votes for party ward candidates), B is the seats in the council and C is the independent ward councillors elected. The IEC's own Seat Calculation Detail reports print a different denominator, B − C − D, where D is "Ward Councillors Seats from Parties with no PR List". D is not in the Act's Item 12 text and its legal basis was not found. Act 3 of 2021 (in force 1 November 2021) added Items 16(3) to 16(9). They recalculate the quota without the party that has excessive seats, using Q' = floor((A − B) / (C − (D + E))) + 1. No court has decided a remainder tie, and no IEC document states a remainder tie-break. One critical caveat: no current consolidated text of Items 12 to 16 could be retrieved. Item 12 and Item 13 wording comes from older consolidations plus the 2021 Gazette amendment, and must be confirmed against a current consolidation before any calculator or legal statement relies on it.

## How sources were graded and what could not be verified

Three grades of source sit behind this report. The first is the Act as published: Act 3 of 2021 in its Government Gazette form, read through the Parliamentary Monitoring Group (PMG) and Parliament copies. The second is LawLibrary/AfricanLII reproductions of the Gazette text with amendments applied. These are not official publications, and each carries the notice that it "is not the latest available version". The third is secondary material such as news reports and search summaries. Where a passage below is quoted it is verbatim from the source named. Where a sentence is explanatory it is paraphrase or inference, and says so.

The central limitation is that **no consolidation of the Act current to 2026 was obtained**. The LawLibrary current page, SAFLII and the gov.za original were all blocked (HTTP 403, connection resets or TLS failures). Item 12 was read in the LawLibrary consolidations "as at 5 August 2002" and "as at 1 March 2001" ([LawLibrary 2002](https://media.lawlibrary.org.za/media/legislation/298866/source_file/57c2031caec55db5/1998-117.pdf); [LawLibrary 2001](https://media.lawlibrary.org.za/media/legislation/298633/source_file/f9f75921138d5e38/1998-117.pdf)), and its words match the "as at 17 April 2009" consolidation ([LawLibrary 2009](https://media.lawlibrary.org.za/media/legislation/299115/source_file/df3b46e236e79c40/1998-117.pdf)). Item 13 and the original Item 16(1) to 16(2) were read in the 2009 consolidation. Items 16(3) to 16(9) come from the Gazette text of Act 3 of 2021 ([PMG](https://static.pmg.org.za/Act_3_of_2021_Municipal_Structures.pdf)). Two further cautions apply. The research notes say item numbering shifted between the 2002 and 2009 consolidations (a forfeited-seats quota at 2002 Item 14(4) is not in the 2009 Item 14, which covers uncontested elections), so numbers cited from the 2002 text should not be assumed to survive. And Items 12 and 16(3) rest on typeset formula images that text extraction drops. The 2002 Item 12 formula was read from a rendered page by the researcher. The Item 16(3) formula was checked visually against the printed Gazette page of Act 3 of 2021.

The case that these texts are still in force on 4 November 2026 is circumstantial but reasonable. Act 3 of 2021 section 33 amends Schedule 1 only at Items 16 (new subitems 3 to 9), 17(1), new 17A, 18(1)(b) and (c), and 20(1), and nothing in Items 12 to 15 ([PMG](https://static.pmg.org.za/Act_3_of_2021_Municipal_Structures.pdf)). The amendment histories printed in Bill B9-2025 and B23-2026 list no Schedule 1 amendment after Act 3 of 2021. The Constitutional Court in November 2025 also quoted Item 13(2) in the form below, which is a useful but second-hand confirmation that the text had not changed by then ([ZACC 26, fn 6](https://collections.concourt.org.za/bitstreams/e2ecb4ca-73ae-43a7-87d6-6fc6f7e44caf/download)).

## Item 12 sets the quota with a three-factor denominator, and the IEC adds a fourth

Item 12(1), read in the 2002 consolidation and matching the 2009 words, says the quota "must be determined in accordance with the following formula (fractions to be disregarded)". The formula is an image, read as A divided by (B − C), plus 1. The text then defines A as "the total number of valid votes cast for all parties, consisting of those cast on the party vote and those cast for ward candidates representing parties", B as "the number of seats in the metro or local council", and C as "the number of independent ward councillors elected in the election" ([LawLibrary 2002](https://media.lawlibrary.org.za/media/legislation/298866/source_file/57c2031caec55db5/1998-117.pdf)). Item 12(2) doubles votes for factor A: where a party's ward candidate is elected unopposed, a vote by a voter registered in that ward for that party "must for the purpose of factor A be counted as two votes" (same source). Item 13(1)(b) repeats the doubling for the per-party counts used in allocation. The doubling attaches only to the unopposed candidate's party in that ward, which is an inference from the text.

The statutory denominator is therefore **B − C**. The IEC's published Seat Calculation Detail reports use something else. The 2021 reports for JB Marks (NW405) and Maluti a Phofung (FS194), and 2016 reports for Bushbuckridge and Ventersdorp/Tlokwe, all print "Q = (A / (B - C- D)) + 1; disregarding fractions", with D labelled "Ward Councillors Seats from Parties with no PR List" ([IEC NW405 2021](https://results.elections.org.za/home/LGEPublicReports/1091/Seat%20Calculation%20Detail/NW/NW405.pdf); [IEC FS194 2021](https://results.elections.org.za/home/LGEPublicReports/1091/Seat%20Calculation%20Detail/FS/FS194.pdf); [IEC MP325 2016](https://www.elections.org.za/content/LGEPublicReports/402/Seat%20Calculation%20Detail/MP/MP325.pdf)). In both 2021 reports opened, D was zero, so the two formulas give the same quota there. For JB Marks, 101,439 / 67 = 1,514.07, which truncates to 1,514, plus 1 gives the printed quota of 1,515. For Maluti a Phofung, 157,298 / 70 = 2,247.11, giving 2,248 (the researcher's arithmetic against the IEC figures). The IEC's voter-education slides, by contrast, print a version with no D: total valid votes divided by (total seats − independents), plus one ([IEC slides](https://www.elections.org.za/content/Documents/Voter-education/Continuous-Voter-Education/How-Council-seats-are-calculated/)).

Two points follow. First, the D in the IEC's quota is an operational adjustment with no source in the Act text read; the plausible rationale (ward seats won by parties with no PR list cannot be offset against a list, so they are removed from the pool) is unsourced. Second, the letter D means something different in Item 16(3), covered below, so a calculator must not share one variable between the two. On the order of operations, because 1 is an integer, floor(x) + 1 equals floor(x + 1), so it does not matter which step the phrase "fractions to be disregarded" attaches to. Party quotients themselves are not truncated: the IEC reports show party votes divided by the integer quota to four decimals (ANC in JB Marks: 48,911 / 1,515 = 32.2845), and the remainder is ranked, consistent with Item 13(2) ([IEC NW405 2021](https://results.elections.org.za/home/LGEPublicReports/1091/Seat%20Calculation%20Detail/NW/NW405.pdf)).

## Item 13(2)(b) breaks equal surpluses by total valid votes

The Act never uses the word "remainder"; it says "surplus". Item 13(1)(a) divides each party's valid votes (party vote plus ward-candidate votes) by the quota, and that whole-number result is the party's entitlement before adjustment. Item 13(2) then reads, verbatim from the 2009 consolidation: "(a) If the calculation in subitem (1) yields a surplus not absorbed by the seats awarded to a party, that surplus must compete with similar surpluses accruing to any other party or parties, and any undistributed seat or seats must be awarded to the party or parties concerned in sequence of the highest surplus. (b) If the surplus for two or more parties is equal the seat must be awarded to the party that obtained the highest number of valid votes." ([LawLibrary 2009](https://media.lawlibrary.org.za/media/legislation/299115/source_file/df3b46e236e79c40/1998-117.pdf)). The Constitutional Court quoted the same two paragraphs in November 2025 ([ZACC 26, fn 6](https://collections.concourt.org.za/bitstreams/e2ecb4ca-73ae-43a7-87d6-6fc6f7e44caf/download)). Item 13(3) then deducts ward seats won from each party's total, leaving the number of list seats. Item 13(4) treats party votes as surpluses if no party is awarded a seat under 13(1).

The tie-break is on the party's valid votes, not on the surplus, so it is a genuine second criterion. The Act does not say whether "valid votes" in 13(2)(b) means the Item 13(1)(a) total (party plus ward votes, with unopposed-ward doubling) or the party vote alone. Reading it as the 13(1)(a) total is the natural interpretation, since that is the figure the Item works with, but it is an interpretation and should be confirmed with an attorney or the IEC. A pending private member's bill, B9-2025, would add 13(2)(c), allowing only a party that already holds at least one seat under 13(1)(a) to compete for surpluses. Parliament lists it at the "Introduction" stage and nothing found shows it passing ([PMG B9-2025](https://static.pmg.org.za/B9-2025-GJ.pdf); [Parliament](https://www.parliament.gov.za/bill/2321873)). B23-2026 contains no reference to Schedule 1 Items 12 to 16 ([Parliament B23-2026](https://www.parliament.gov.za/storage/app/media/Bills/2026/B23-2026_Municipal_Structures_AB.pdf)). Neither is law as at 1 November 2026, so far as could be established.

The same Constitutional Court judgment supports reading the votes-based rule as deliberate. Socialist Agenda of Dispossessed Africans v Minister of CoGTA [2025] ZACC 26 concerned a lot-draw for the Fetakgomo Tubatse executive committee, where equal surpluses were broken by lot even though the losing party had more votes (3,667 against 3,321). The Court declined to confirm a declaration of invalidity against section 43(2)(c), calling lot "a robust and seemingly peculiar mechanism chosen by Parliament". It contrasted elective bodies, saying the legislature "clearly contemplated the possibility of breaking deadlocks by having regard to votes cast", and cited Schedule 1 Items 13(2), 16(4) and 17(5) ([ZACC 26, paras 4-8, 45-46, fn 33](https://collections.concourt.org.za/bitstreams/e2ecb4ca-73ae-43a7-87d6-6fc6f7e44caf/download)). Because the Court was not deciding a council-seat tie, this comment is obiter.

## Item 16 and the 2021 amendment recalculate the quota without the excessive party

Before 2021, Item 16 had only two subitems, read verbatim in the 2009 consolidation: "(1) If, through the election of ward candidates, a party listed on the part of the ballot paper for parties has obtained a number of seats that is equal to, or greater than the total number of seats in the council to which it is entitled under item 13, that party must not be allocated any seats from its list of party candidates. (2) The seats of ward candidates are not affected." ([LawLibrary 2009](https://media.lawlibrary.org.za/media/legislation/299115/source_file/df3b46e236e79c40/1998-117.pdf)). Act 3 of 2021 section 33(a) added subitems (3) to (9). Section 39 left commencement to presidential proclamation, and the proclamation fixed 1 November 2021 ([PMG Act 3 of 2021](https://static.pmg.org.za/Act_3_of_2021_Municipal_Structures.pdf); [gov.za](https://www.gov.za/documents/local-government-municipal-structures-amendment-act-3-2021-1-jun-2021-0000), Gazette 45305 of 11 October 2021).

Item 16(3) orders "a new quota of votes for a seat" to be determined, fractions disregarded. The formula, checked on the printed Gazette page, is **Q' = floor((A − B) / (C − (D + E))) + 1**. Verbatim definitions follow. A is "the total number of valid votes cast for all parties, consisting of those cast on the party vote and those cast for ward candidates representing parties". B is "the total number of votes cast for the party with excessive seats, both on the party vote and for ward candidates representing parties". C is "the number of seats in the council". D is "the number of seats awarded to the party with excessive seats". E is "the number of independent ward councillors elected" ([PMG Act 3 of 2021](https://static.pmg.org.za/Act_3_of_2021_Municipal_Structures.pdf)). The subitems that follow mirror Item 13 but exclude the excessive party. Item 16(4)(a) divides each other party's votes by the new quota. Item 16(4)(b) repeats the highest-surplus competition. Item 16(4)(c) reads "If the surplus for two or more parties is equal, the seat must be awarded to the party that received the highest number of valid votes". Item 16(5) doubles unopposed-ward votes for factors A and B. Item 16(6) deducts ward seats won. Item 16(7) treats votes as surpluses if no party gets a seat. Item 16(8) selects candidates as in Item 13(5). Item 16(9) provides for forfeiture of unfilled seats and repetition of the process until all seats are filled or all listed candidates are placed.

Two features need care. The tie wording is the same in substance in Item 13(2)(b) and 16(4)(c), but differs in one word ("obtained" against "received"), which is not a substantive difference on its face. The Constitutional Court's footnote quoted 16(4)(b) without the tie sentence, but that appears to be a quotation choice, since the Gazette text of 16(4)(c) carries it ([ZACC 26, fn 6](https://collections.concourt.org.za/bitstreams/e2ecb4ca-73ae-43a7-87d6-6fc6f7e44caf/download)). Note also that D in 16(3) (seats awarded to the excessive party) is a different quantity from the IEC's D in the Item 12 quota (ward seats of parties with no PR list), and that E in 16(3) is the same quantity as C in Item 12.

## When surplus and votes are both equal, the Act is silent and practice is undocumented

A full tie, with equal surpluses and equal valid votes, is addressed nowhere in Items 12 to 16. Searches of the 2009 consolidation and of Act 3 of 2021 found no lot, no Commission discretion and no tertiary criterion in Items 13(2) or 16(4). The Act does use lot elsewhere: Item 8(2) of Schedule 1 for equal votes between ward candidates, section 43(2)(c) for executive committee surpluses, and Schedule 3 for office-bearers. The Court notes that the Act "does not specify the exact procedure for casting lots" ([ZACC 26, paras 25, 40](https://collections.concourt.org.za/bitstreams/e2ecb4ca-73ae-43a7-87d6-6fc6f7e44caf/download)). Extending lot to Items 13 or 16 would be borrowing, not reading, the statute, so a calculator should flag a full tie as unresolved rather than invent a draw.

On practice, nothing on remainder ties was found. The IEC's 2021 Electoral System deck says only that "any remaining seats are allocated in order of the highest remainder" and cites Schedule 1, with no tie language ([IEC deck](https://www.elections.org.za/content/Documents/Voter-education/Continuous-Voter-Education/How-Council-seats-are-calculated/)). That omits the Item 13(2)(b) tie-break, an inference from the deck's text. No regulation, directive or results-system specification was found. No court decision on an actual Schedule 1 remainder tie was found. The Electoral Court judgments of 2004 (national highest-remainder allocation) and 2022 (Academic Congress Union v IEC, a seat-count discrepancy) were identified only from snippets and not read. The documented ties are of other kinds: the Fetakgomo Tubatse executive committee lot of 23 November 2021 (primary), a 2011 Mutale ward tie of 823 votes each resolved by coin toss by the IEC, and a Modimolle-Mookgophong mayoral tie in December 2021 decided by coin toss under Schedule 3 ([Business Report](https://busrep.co.za/the-star/soweto/ward-candidate-loses-by-flip-of-a-coin-1073262); [Pretoria News](https://pretorianews.co.za/news/2021-12-06-da-takes-mayoral-position-in-modimolle-mookgophong-through-coin-toss/), both secondary). A Ditsobotla ward coin toss in 2006 is known only from an unverified search summary and is not relied on. Absence of reports does not show that remainder ties never occurred, and a votes-based tie-break would rarely be newsworthy.

## What the Act leaves open

The Act leaves several interpretive points open. These should be treated as gaps, not settled rules.

| Open point | What the text says | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Which "valid votes" count in the 13(2)(b) and 16(4)(c) tie rule | "highest number of valid votes" only | Reading as party plus ward votes (the 13(1)(a) total, with unopposed doubling) is an interpretation; confirm with an attorney or the IEC |
| Meaning of D in 16(3) | "the number of seats awarded to the party with excessive seats" | Could mean the party's Item 13 entitlement, its ward seats won, or seats otherwise "awarded"; the Act does not say. The choice changes the denominator |
| More than one excessive party | 16(3) and 16(4) speak of "the party with excessive seats" in the singular | Not addressed; whether B and D are summed across parties is unstated |
| Secondary excess after recalculation | 16(9) repeats the process after forfeiture of unfilled list seats, "subject to subitem (1)" | Whether a different party can become excessive under the new quota, and how Q' is then recomputed, is not spelled out |
| Full tie (equal surplus and equal votes) | Silent | No statutory, regulatory or IEC rule found; do not assume lot |
| Basis for the IEC's D in the Item 12 quota | Not in Item 12 | No legal source found; no 2021 report with D above zero was identified |
| Current text | Older consolidations plus 2021 Gazette | Needs confirmation against a current consolidation or Gazette |

## Conclusion

The practical finding is a layered one. The Act itself resolves a first-level tie cleanly and the same way in Items 13 and 16, by votes. It then stops, and nothing the researchers could find from the IEC or the courts fills the gap. The only judicial comment, the Constitutional Court's 2025 SADA judgment, supports reading the votes rule as a deliberate legislative choice for elective bodies while upholding lot for executive posts, but it never decided a council-seat remainder tie. A system that models the 2026 allocation should therefore implement the votes tie-break, flag a full tie for human and legal resolution, and keep the IEC's D adjustment separate from the Item 16 variables.

Two assumptions remain uncertain and are worth settling before 4 November 2026. One is the current consolidated wording of Items 12 and 13, which has been inferred from 2001 to 2009 consolidations and a 2021 amendment that does not touch them. The other is the IEC's practice on D in the quota and on D in the Item 16 recalculation, where the Act's wording leaves room for different counts. A written confirmation from the IEC on those two points would close the largest risks.
